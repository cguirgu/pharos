**Subject:** Request for permission — Synaxarium text in a free Coptic Orthodox app

---

To the Diocese of the Southern United States,

My name is Christina Guirguis. I build **Coptic Daily Companion**, a free and open-source iPhone
app for daily Coptic Orthodox practice: the fasts and feasts of the Coptic calendar, the Agpeya,
the daily readings, and a personal rule of life. The source is public at
github.com/cguirgu/pharos.

I am writing to request written permission to display the Diocese's English translation of the
Synaxarium in the app — the reading for each day, shown one day at a time.

I am aware of the Diocese's terms of use, which permit personal and non-commercial use of
published material and require written permission otherwise. That is precisely why I am writing
before doing anything rather than after.

**What I would do with it:**

- Display the day's Synaxarium reading in-app, one day at a time. No bulk export, no separate
  republication, no resale.
- The app is free: no subscription, no advertising, and the Synaxarium would never be paywalled.
- Attribution in whatever form the Diocese requires, on the screen where the reading appears.
- Immediate removal at the Diocese's request, in the next release, no explanation required.
- Any correction the Diocese sends, applied.

If the Diocese would prefer to grant this under specific conditions — particular attribution
wording, a review of how the text is presented before release, a limit on scope, or a formal
licence agreement — I will meet them. And if the answer is simply no, that is an entirely
acceptable answer; I would direct users to Coptic Reader instead, which is what many of them
already use alongside this app.

I should also say plainly that Coptic Reader is the reference standard here and I am not trying
to replace it. This app is built around a rule of life and the daily rhythm of fasting and
reading; the Synaxarium would be one part of a morning, not a service book.

Thank you for your time, and for the translation work the Diocese has made available to the
English-speaking Church.

Asking your prayers,

Christina Guirguis
